\# Dashboard Guide



The HR Analytics Dashboard provides an interactive overview of employee attrition and workforce characteristics.



The dashboard combines key performance indicators with visual analysis across employee demographics, job roles, education, salary, gender, and years at the company.



\## Dashboard Filters



\### Department Filter



The department selector at the top of the dashboard allows the analysis to be viewed across different departments:



\- Human Resources

\- Research \& Development

\- Sales



Selecting a department updates the dashboard visuals to provide a more focused view of the selected workforce segment.



\## Key Performance Indicators



\### Employee Count



Displays the total number of employees represented in the selected dataset or filter context.



\### Attrition



Shows the total number of employees recorded as having left the organization.



\### Attrition Rate



Represents the proportion of employees who have left the organization relative to the overall employee population.



\### Average Age



Displays the average age of employees in the selected data.



\### Average Salary



Shows the average salary value for the employee population represented by the dashboard.



\### Average Years at Company



Displays the average number of years employees have spent with the organization.



\## Dashboard Visualizations



\### Attrition by Gender



Shows the number of attrition cases across male and female employees.



This visualization helps compare the distribution of attrition between genders.



\### Attrition by Age



Displays attrition cases across different age groups:



\- 18–25

\- 26–35

\- 36–45

\- 46–55

\- 55+



This helps identify the age groups with the highest concentration of employee attrition.



\### Attrition by Roles



Displays the number of attrition cases across different job roles.



The visualization helps identify roles with relatively higher or lower numbers of employee exits.



\### Job Role and Job Satisfaction



The table provides a breakdown of job roles across job satisfaction ratings from 1 to 4, along with the total number of employees represented for each role.



This allows job satisfaction levels to be compared across different roles.



\### Attrition by Education



Shows the distribution of attrition across educational backgrounds, including:



\- Life Sciences

\- Medical

\- Marketing

\- Technical Degree

\- Other



This helps identify which educational categories represent a larger share of the observed attrition.



\### Attrition by Years at Company



Shows how attrition varies according to employee tenure.



The visualization helps identify points in an employee's career where attrition appears to be more concentrated.



\### Attrition by Salary



Groups attrition cases into salary ranges:



\- Up to 5K

\- 5K–10K

\- 10K–15K

\- 15K+



This helps examine the distribution of employee attrition across different salary bands.



\## Interactive Analysis



The dashboard is designed to allow users to explore the data interactively.



Users can select a department and examine how the displayed workforce metrics and visualizations change based on the selected filter context.



\## Purpose of the Dashboard



The dashboard is intended to support exploratory HR analysis by providing a consolidated view of employee attrition patterns.



It can help HR teams identify areas that may require further investigation, such as:



\- High-attrition age groups

\- Job roles with higher attrition

\- Salary bands with higher attrition

\- Differences in attrition by gender

\- Education categories associated with higher attrition

\- Attrition patterns across employee tenure

\- Department-level differences



The dashboard focuses on identifying patterns in the available data rather than establishing direct causes of employee attrition.
