\# Key Insights



The HR Analytics Dashboard highlights several patterns in employee attrition across demographics, job roles, salary ranges, education, and employee tenure.



\## 1. Overall Attrition



\- The dataset contains \*\*1,470 employees\*\*.

\- \*\*237 employees\*\* are recorded as having left the organization.

\- The overall attrition rate is \*\*16.1%\*\*.

\- The average employee age is \*\*37 years\*\*.

\- The average salary is approximately \*\*6.5K\*\*.

\- The average employee tenure is \*\*7 years\*\*.



\## 2. Attrition by Age



The \*\*26–35 age group\*\* records the highest number of attrition cases, with \*\*116 employees\*\*, followed by:



\- 18–25: 44 employees

\- 36–45: 43 employees

\- 46–55: 26 employees

\- 55+: 8 employees



This indicates that attrition is particularly concentrated among employees in the 26–35 age group.



\## 3. Attrition by Job Role



Among the job roles displayed in the dashboard:



\- \*\*Sales Executive\*\* has the highest attrition count at \*\*57 employees\*\*.

\- \*\*Research Scientist\*\* follows with \*\*47 employees\*\*.

\- \*\*Sales Representative\*\* accounts for \*\*33 employees\*\*.

\- \*\*Research Director\*\* has the lowest displayed attrition count at \*\*2 employees\*\*.



The results indicate that sales-oriented roles account for a significant share of the observed attrition.



\## 4. Attrition by Salary Range



The largest number of attrition cases is concentrated in the \*\*Up to 5K\*\* salary range, with \*\*163 employees\*\*.



The remaining attrition cases are distributed across:



\- 5K–10K: 49 employees

\- 10K–15K: 20 employees

\- 15K+: 5 employees



This shows a strong concentration of observed attrition among employees in the lowest salary band represented in the dashboard.



\## 5. Attrition by Gender



The dashboard records:



\- \*\*Male:\*\* 140 attrition cases

\- \*\*Female:\*\* 79 attrition cases



Male employees therefore account for a larger share of the observed attrition cases.



\## 6. Attrition by Education



The education analysis shows the following distribution of attrition:



\- \*\*Life Sciences:\*\* 38%

\- \*\*Medical:\*\* 27%

\- \*\*Marketing:\*\* 15%

\- \*\*Technical Degree:\*\* 14%

\- \*\*Other:\*\* 5%



Employees with a Life Sciences educational background represent the largest share of the attrition distribution shown in the dashboard.



\## 7. Attrition by Years at Company



The dashboard shows that attrition varies considerably across employee tenure.



The highest visible peak occurs around the earlier years of employment, with a notable peak of \*\*59 attrition cases\*\*. Attrition then fluctuates across subsequent years of tenure.



This suggests that employee turnover is not evenly distributed throughout an employee's tenure and that the earlier stages of employment may require closer attention.



\## 8. Overall Business Observations



Based on the dashboard, several areas stand out for further HR investigation:



\- Employees aged \*\*26–35\*\* represent the largest age group among attrition cases.

\- \*\*Sales Executive\*\* is the job role with the highest observed attrition count.

\- The \*\*Up to 5K\*\* salary band contains the largest number of attrition cases.

\- Male employees account for more observed attrition cases than female employees.

\- \*\*Life Sciences\*\* represents the largest education category within the displayed attrition distribution.

\- Attrition shows noticeable variation across employee tenure.



\## Important Note



These observations describe patterns visible in the dataset and dashboard. They indicate areas that may require further investigation but do not by themselves establish the underlying causes of employee attrition.
